# Candidates

Tracked list of candidate hypotheses found by the survey of the cached literature (`docs/survey.md`), and of
records that belong on an existing card. A candidate is not a card: the founder decides which to draft, through
the manual card process. Case definitions below were read from abstracts only and are unverified until a card is
drafted. Status is one of `candidate`, `to draft`, `drafted as <slug>`, `declined: <reason>`; set it by hand.
The weekly report's "possible new hypotheses" items are added here by hand when the founder reads the report.
Refuted hypotheses are not candidates; they are listed with the reason in `docs/pm/excluded-hypotheses.md`.

## Negative mood and emotion regulation shape symptom severity and symptom reporting

- Source heading: Affect
- Mechanism: Low mood, anxiety, rumination and beliefs about emotions are proposed to amplify or maintain fatigue, cognitive complaints and symptom reporting in ME/CFS and related functional syndromes.
- Records: 6 cohort studies, 4 reviews (of 29 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:25575044: CDC_unspecified, n=118. In 118 adults meeting 1994 CDC criteria, higher depression and anxiety were associated with greater fatigue severity and more cognitive complaints in regression models (associations, not causal).
  - pmid:30920465: unverified, n=81. In 81 patients with fibromyalgia and/or chronic fatigue syndrome, affect-induced symptom reporting was unrelated to distorted dyspnea perception, suggesting context-dependent rather than trait-like symptom distortion.
  - pmid:20100398: unverified, n=33. Thirty-three CFS participants showed greater mood fluctuation than 33 controls after rumination or distraction induction, with no attentional or interpretation bias.
  - pmid:20159215: unverified, n=121. People with CFS (n=121) scored higher than 73 healthy controls on a new Beliefs about Emotions Scale, which correlated with depression, anxiety and fatigue.
  - pmid:26715232: Oxford, n=27. In 27 adults meeting Oxford criteria, subjective but not actigraphy-defined sleep predicted next-day fatigue, partly mediated by negative mood on waking.
  - pmid:24826505: unverified, n=68. In 68 women with CFS, emotional factors predicted verbal memory dysfunction and fatigue predicted attention and executive impairment.
- Reviews: pmid:18267050, pmid:19768898, pmid:15023475, pmid:23261014
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: yes
- Why: Only 25575044 carries the rule, via '1994 CDC criteria' (written CDC_unspecified; it may be Fukuda, not stated by name). Its findings are cross-sectional associations and cannot show direction. 26715232 is Oxford-only (broad). The rest name no case definition. The reviews are psychological or functional-syndrome frameworks, some with treatment proposals that are outside the atlas. The founder should decide whether a psychological hypothesis fits the card set.
- Status: candidate
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Increased serotonergic signalling

- Source heading: Affect
- Mechanism: An upregulated serotonin system is proposed to contribute to fatigue and symptoms in ME/CFS.
- Records: 1 cohort studies, 0 reviews (of 29 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:25227994: CDC_unspecified, n=5. In a pilot cross-over study of 5 women with CFS (CDC criteria), acute tryptophan depletion lowered the plasma tryptophan ratio by 96% without significant change in fatigue, depression or concentration.
- Reviews: none
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: yes
- Why: Only 25227994 (n=5, CDC criteria not further specified) supports this. A negative pilot too small to settle the question. The abstract mentions a prior ondansetron trial without benefit but gives no PMID. Might alternatively sit on the tryptophan-kynurenine-metabolism card; founder to decide.
- Status: candidate
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Borna disease virus infection in chronic fatigue syndrome

- Source heading: Antibodies, Viral
- Mechanism: Infection with the neurotropic Borna disease virus contributes to or starts chronic fatigue syndrome in some patients.
- Records: 2 cohort studies, 0 reviews (of 29 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:9396313: unverified, n=89. In Japanese CFS patients, BDV antibodies were found in 34% (30/89) by immunoblot and BDV RNA in 12% (7/57) by PCR; the abstract does not name controls or a case definition.
  - pmid:24170181: unverified, n not stated. BDV RNA in blood cells was found in 12.7% of CFS patients, higher than healthy controls, in a mixed neuropsychiatric study from western China.
- Reviews: none
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: no
- Why: Neither abstract names a case definition. 9396313 is typed as a review in PubMed but reports original prevalence data. Unreplicated and from a small number of regional groups; the abstract of 9396313 itself says a single etiologic role is unlikely.

## Autonomic nervous system imbalance (reduced vagal tone, raised sympathetic activity)

- Source heading: Blood Pressure
- Mechanism: Control of heart rate and blood pressure by the autonomic nervous system is altered in ME/CFS and post-infectious states, with less parasympathetic and more sympathetic activity.
- Records: 8 cohort studies, 4 reviews (of 26 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:29246267: IOM2015, n=8. In eight patients meeting IOM 2015 criteria and eight tired controls, overnight recordings showed lower parasympathetic tone in deep sleep, higher sympathetic tone and higher nocturnal systolic and mean blood pressure.
  - pmid:41437251: unverified, n=112. In 112 CFS patients and 61 healthy controls, beat-to-beat measures (lower cardiac vagal tone, lower stroke volume) fed a classifier that separated the groups; no external validation is described.
  - pmid:41598987: unverified, n=100. Among 100 seropositive Coxiella burnetii patients with polymorphic symptoms, dysautonomia or ME/CFS and 56 controls, autonomic dysfunction (mainly parasympathetic) and blood pressure variability on tilt were more common.
  - pmid:30908516: unverified, n=42. A network analysis in 42 patients and 9 controls found the autonomic network structure differed from controls; the authors call for replication.
  - pmid:27114901: unverified, n=25. In 25 CFS subjects and 25 controls, blood pressure and heart rate correlated abnormally with MRI of brainstem vasomotor and hypothalamic regions.
  - pmid:28339438: unverified, n=20. In 20 women with ME/CFS and 20 controls, reduced parasympathetic reactivation after exercise was associated with impaired exercise-induced analgesia.
  - pmid:30876666: unverified, n=191. In 191 youth (42 healthy controls), physician-measured orthostatic changes correlated weakly with self-reported orthostatic symptoms and did not detect them.
  - pmid:29855991: unverified, n=28. In 14 women with CFS and 14 controls, no group difference in cerebral blood flow or heart rate variability was found; higher values of each were associated with lower fatigue severity within the CFS group.
- Reviews: pmid:9857114, pmid:17561705, pmid:34474739, pmid:29417255
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: yes
- Why: 29246267 carries the rule (IOM 2015 named) but has only eight patients. Most others name no case definition. 30876666 and 29855991 are weak or null for this hypothesis. 41598987 is a post-infection cohort in which ME/CFS is only one of the presentations.
- Status: drafted as autonomic-imbalance (founder ruling 2026-11-08)
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Small heart, low cardiac output and reduced blood volume regulation

- Source heading: Blood Pressure
- Mechanism: People with ME/CFS may have reduced cardiac preload, smaller left ventricle and lower stroke volume, with down-regulated blood-volume hormones, contributing to orthostatic intolerance and symptoms.
- Records: 2 cohort studies, 1 reviews (of 26 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:24736946: unverified, n=40. In 40 ME patients versus 40 controls, left ventricular size, stroke volume index, cardiac index and blood pressure were lower, and orthostatic intolerance was found in 97% of those who could stand.
  - pmid:27401397: unverified, n=18. In 18 ME patients versus 15 controls, plasma renin activity, aldosterone and antidiuretic hormone were lower alongside lower cardiac output; an open desmopressin trial in 10 patients was uncontrolled.
- Reviews: pmid:34474739
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: no
- Why: Both cohort studies come from one research group, are small, and name no case definition in the abstract, so the inclusion rule is not met on abstracts. The review 34474739 reports reduced stroke volume and cardiac MRI findings in ME/CFS.
- Status: candidate
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Disrupted daily (circadian) rhythms of activity, body temperature and light exposure

- Source heading: Circadian Rhythm
- Mechanism: The body's 24-hour rhythms of rest-activity, skin temperature and sleep-wake timing are altered or unstable in ME/CFS and post-COVID fatigue, and this is linked to symptoms and to vascular markers.
- Records: 6 cohort studies, 2 reviews (of 30 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:29874259: Fukuda, n=10. In 10 women meeting both Fukuda and Canadian criteria and 10 controls, actigraphy showed lower daily activity, relative amplitude and activity-rhythm stability in patients, and lower nocturnal distal skin temperature only in winter.
  - pmid:39037125: unverified, n=54. A prospective study of 31 ME/CFS and 23 post-COVID ME/CFS patients and 31 controls found lower activity and worse sleep in patients, with lipid profile linked to disturbed temperature rhythm and higher endothelin-1 and VCAM-1.
  - pmid:36902264: unverified, n=67. In 67 female ME/CFS patients and 48 controls, endothelin-1 and VCAM-1 were higher in patients, and endothelin-1 was associated with stability of the skin temperature rhythm.
  - pmid:42399727: unverified, n=100. In a cross-sectional study of 100 ME/CFS patients and 56 controls, irregular or nocturnal light exposure was associated with worse fatigue, sleep and quality of life in patients; causality cannot be inferred.
  - pmid:21532961: unverified, n=15. A case-control study of 15 CFS patients and 15 controls found no between-group differences in sleep, activity or cortisol pattern, and concluded there was no evidence of circadian disturbance.
  - pmid:18031961: unverified, n=12. Actigraphy in 12 children with CFS found irregular or delayed sleep phase patterns, longer total sleep and lower activity than controls.
- Reviews: pmid:42320559, pmid:7795892
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: yes
- Why: PMID 29874259 carries the rule (abstract names Fukuda and Canadian criteria) but has only n=10 and found no difference in rhythm stability or phase, so the evidence is small. Larger cohorts (39037125, 36902264, 42399727) do not name a case definition in the abstract, and 21532961 found no circadian disturbance, so the findings are mixed. Several cohorts seem to come from one research group and may overlap. The Fukuda value is entered for 29874259; the abstract also names Canadian criteria (CCC). The cell study 37393850 (patient serum on mouse fibroblasts) is left out as not a patient cohort.
- Status: to draft (founder ruling 2026-11-08)
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Murine leukaemia virus-related retrovirus (XMRV/MLV) infection in ME/CFS

- Source heading: DNA, Viral
- Mechanism: A human gammaretrovirus (XMRV or related MLV) infects people with ME/CFS and contributes to the illness.
- Records: 9 cohort studies, 1 reviews (of 35 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:25092471: unverified, n=72. A blinded multi-site study found murine retroviral DNA signatures in 5/72 CFS and 2/37 healthy subjects (no difference), consistent with endogenous mouse sequences in reagents.
  - pmid:21342521: other, n=45. In 45 CFS cases (6+ months of post-exertional malaise and high disability) and 42 controls, PCR and Western blot found no XMRV or MuLV.
  - pmid:21628393: unverified, n=61. No XMRV or MLV was found in 61 CFS patients, 43 of whom had earlier been reported XMRV-positive; reagent contamination was identified.
  - pmid:22114717: unverified, n=58. No XMRV/MLV nucleic acids, infectious virus or antibodies were found in 58 Canadian CFS patients and 57 controls.
  - pmid:21925693: unverified, n=72. No XMRV was detected by PCR, serology or co-culture in Quebec CFS patients (72 by PCR, 113 by co-culture).
  - pmid:21472770: unverified, n=43. Cerebrospinal fluid from 43 CFS patients had no XMRV or multiple other common viruses by PCR.
  - pmid:22842604: unverified, n=12. Two of 12 sequential CFS patients were positive for XMRV/MLV sequences versus none of 40 controls; the authors say this does not confirm a high frequency.
  - pmid:22363509: unverified, n=4. Viral sequences in plasma of 4 CFS patients earlier reported XMRV-positive behaved like contaminating mouse endogenous MLV.
  - pmid:22629404: unverified, n not stated. MLV-like gag sequences were found by PCR in human DNA preparations from New York CFS/ME patients and controls, but the authors could not conclude they originated in the blood samples.
- Reviews: pmid:21501825
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: no
- Why: No abstract names a recognised case definition. The records mostly report non-replication and laboratory contamination (see also 21628392, 21352548 and 21697491, which are laboratory studies, not patient cohorts). This is useful as a refuted or not-replicated hypothesis; 22842604 is the only one reporting positives. Founder decides whether it belongs as a card, and the original positive report is not in this file.
- Status: declined: refuted, no card (founder ruling 2026-11-08; docs/pm/excluded-hypotheses.md)
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Altered immune-cell gene expression in blood at rest

- Source heading: Gene Expression Profiling
- Mechanism: Blood immune cells of people with ME/CFS carry a distinct gene-expression pattern, for example in B cell, monocyte or T cell genes, at baseline.
- Records: 6 cohort studies, 2 reviews (of 28 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:28494812: other, n=29. A study of 29 adolescents with CFS (broad definition: 3 months of unexplained fatigue) and 18 controls found 176 differentially expressed whole-blood genes, with enrichment suggesting altered B cell differentiation and enhanced innate antiviral responses.
  - pmid:38232699: unverified, n not stated. Single-cell RNA sequencing of immune cells found classical monocyte dysregulation at baseline in ME/CFS patients, with the fraction of altered cells correlating with severity.
  - pmid:36284352: unverified, n=166. In 166 self-reported ME/CFS patients and 83 controls using self-collected fingerstick blood, six T cell, B cell and autoimmunity-related genes were higher in bedridden than in mild-to-moderate patients.
  - pmid:33633136: unverified, n=93. A re-analysis of public PBMC expression data from 93 patients and 25 controls found a 23-gene signature that separated cases from controls (AUC 0.92).
  - pmid:30119681: unverified, n=44. Three very long non-coding RNAs (NTT, MIAT, EmX2OS) were higher in PBMCs of 44 ME/CFS patients than in controls, and two rose with severity.
  - pmid:28172519: unverified, n=25. A blinded RNA-seq study of 25 CFS patients, 13 patients with alternatively diagnosed chronic Lyme and 25 controls found no significantly differentially expressed genes in CFS, outside flares.
- Reviews: pmid:24974723, pmid:16515400
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: no
- Why: No listed cohort abstract names a recognised strict case definition (one is a broad definition, the rest are unverified), so the rule is not met on abstracts. Findings conflict: 28172519 found no differential genes, 28494812 found 176. Gene lists differ between studies. Full texts needed to check case definitions.
- Status: candidate
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Abnormal blood-cell gene-expression response to exercise (post-exertional malaise)

- Source heading: Gene Expression Profiling
- Mechanism: After an exertion challenge, immune cells of ME/CFS patients show a different transcriptional response from controls, possibly underlying post-exertional malaise.
- Records: 5 cohort studies, 1 reviews (of 28 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:36769022: unverified, n not stated. In female ME/CFS patients, PBMCs showed no significant change at maximal exertion but dysregulated immune signalling pathways during recovery, unlike controls.
  - pmid:40372680: unverified, n not stated. PBMC RNA sequencing before and after an exercise challenge compared ME/CFS patients with controls; the abstract reports no specific results.
  - pmid:30897114: unverified, n=14. In 14 ME/CFS patients and 11 controls, whole-blood RNA-seq around two days of cardiopulmonary exercise found only 6 differentially expressed genes, none immune-related, and no viral reactivation signal.
  - pmid:32291908: unverified, n not stated. PBMC microRNA expression under an exercise challenge differed between males and females with ME/CFS.
  - pmid:38232699: unverified, n not stated. Single-cell RNA sequencing after exercise suggested improper platelet activation in patients, with minimal change elsewhere in the immune system.
- Reviews: pmid:24974723
- Meets inclusion rule on abstracts: no
- Why: None of the abstracts names a case definition. The 2019 study (30897114) did not find an exercise-linked immune transcriptional change, while the 2023 study (36769022) did, so the evidence is mixed. May overlap with the existing exertion/energy card; founder to decide. The 2014 review reports level B evidence for an altered immune response to exercise.
- Status: candidate
- Date: 2026-11-08

## Altered immune-cell gene expression signature in blood

- Source heading: Gene Expression Regulation
- Mechanism: Blood-cell transcriptomes in ME/CFS show differences in immune pathways (for example B cell, T cell, NK cell and innate antiviral signalling) that may reflect immune dysregulation.
- Records: 3 cohort studies, 0 reviews (of 27 records read)
- Cohort studies (case definition read from the abstract only):
  - pmid:39394558: unverified, n=4. Single-cell RNA sequencing of PBMCs from 4 ME/CFS patients and 4 controls found altered immune cell proportions, signs of B cell overactivity, reduced NK cell cytotoxicity and a possible ESRRA-APP-CD74 pathway signal.
