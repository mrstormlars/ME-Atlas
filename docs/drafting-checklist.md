# Drafting checklist

Read by the `card-drafter` before it writes operations and by the `card-reviewer` before it judges them. Each line is an error the reviews of the first five hand-drafted cards found again and again. Check every operation against all nine.

1. **Within-group change is not a between-group difference.** "Heart rate fell from day 1 to day 2 in patients" is a change inside one group. Say "between patients and controls" only when the source compares the two groups, and say which one the source reports.
2. **Derived numbers are not reported numbers.** A percentage, a difference or a sum that you computed is not what the paper reported. Print the paper's own number, or say "calculated from the reported counts".
3. **Only the rubric's quality criteria.** Case definition, sample size and a control group, as listed in spec section 3 (with preregistration and conflicting results). Do not add criteria such as "blinded", "multi-centre" or "peer-reviewed" to justify a level or to dismiss a study.
4. **A quote says what it says.** A case-definition quote is used for the case definition only. Do not use it as evidence for the design, the sample size or the finding. Check that the quote means the definition you recorded; "diagnosed by criteria" does not name which.
5. **Order of studies matters.** An earlier study cannot be called a repeat of a later one. A replication comes after the original, uses a separate cohort and comes from a different group.
6. **Association is not cause.** "Was lower in patients" and "was associated with" are findings. "Causes", "leads to" and "explains" need an intervention study (level 4) or the whole causal case (level 5).
7. **Label what has not been repeated.** A single study, or a finding no independent group has reproduced, is called unreplicated in the text. It never reads as established.
8. **Sources must match the sentence.** A paragraph's `sources` are the studies its sentences rest on, no more and no fewer. Do not list a study the paragraph does not mention, and do not leave out one it does.
9. **Measurement n is not study n.** If the outcome was measured in 39 of 48 patients, the card says so. Sample size for comparing quality (spec section 3) is the number in the measurement that bears on the finding. Use `n_patients` and `n_controls` for that number and state the whole study's size in the text when it differs.
